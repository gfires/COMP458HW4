⏺ Short answer: yes, for this problem. The ground state of H is the optimal cut.
  It's a different ansatz from QAOA, though.

  Why they're the same problem
  - H = Σ wᵢⱼ ZᵢZⱼ has all positive weights, so energy is lowest when neighbors
    have opposite spins.
  - MaxCut's cost is C = Σ wᵢⱼ(1 − ZᵢZⱼ)/2 = 3.5 − H/2 (the total weight is
    7.0), so minimizing H maximizes the cut.
  - H is diagonal in the Z basis, so the ground state is a computational basis
    state. A brute-force check gives:
    - Best cut: {0,2} | {1,3}, with weight 5.7.
    - Ground energy: 7 − 2·5.7 = −4.4.
    - The bitstrings 0101 and 1010 are degenerate (the cut and its complement).

  VQE vs. QAOA
  - They share the objective and differ in the circuit.
  - QAOA uses problem-structured layers: exp(−iγH) followed by an X-mixer
    exp(−iβΣXᵢ).
  - Your spec asks for a hardware-efficient VQE ansatz (Ry/Rz plus CNOT). That
    is what we build, and it's still a valid way to solve MaxCut.
  - Because H is diagonal, entanglement isn't strictly needed. The CNOTs are
    there to satisfy the spec.
  - The optimizer may land on a superposition of 0101 and 1010. That's fine,
    since either one decodes to the same cut.

  Proposed structure for the new file (e.g. vqe_maxcut.py)
  1. HAMILTONIAN_TERMS: a dict {(i,j): w}, plus a helper that builds the
     cirq.PauliSum from it.
  2. build_ansatz(qubits, params, layers):
     - Each layer applies Ry(θ) and Rz(φ) on every qubit, then a CNOT chain
       (0→1→2→3).
     - There is a final rotation layer after the last CNOTs.
     - params has shape (layers, n, 2).
     - Use sympy symbols or cirq.resolve_parameters so the circuit is built
       once.
  3. energy(params):
     - Run cirq.Simulator().simulate(...) to get the final state vector.
     - Take ⟨H⟩ with PauliSum.expectation_from_state_vector.
     - Return a real float.
  4. optimize():
     - Use scipy.optimize.minimize (COBYLA or L-BFGS-B) with several random
       restarts, since the landscape has local minima.
     - Keep the best result and record the energy history.
  5. decode(state):
     - Take the highest-probability bitstring.
     - Compute its cut weight and compare it with the brute-force optimum (5.7,
       E = −4.4).
  6. A __main__ block that prints the energy, the bitstring, the partition and
     the cut value.
